How this estimate was made—and its limits

Estimate as of 12 September 2026, through cumulative round 35. Round 36 is pending and excluded. This is an estimate of demonstrated recall, not general knowledge about a country, a probability, or a retention test after a known break. No self-reported prior knowledge was used.

All 195 countries were identified in both directions during lessons, sometimes after corrections or omission prompts. This ranking emphasizes the subsequent cumulative tests: 30 forward rounds (360 item attempts) and 5 reverse rounds (60 item attempts). Immediate correction drills do not add successful mixed checks. Accepted spelling variants count as correct; recognizable spelling slips are separated from missing, incomplete or substantially distorted answers.

Countries are sorted by band, then alphabetically, ignoring accents. The strongest band requires repeated clean mixed forward retrieval: at least three consecutive correct answers, or at least two with no cumulative forward errors. The next band includes correctly demonstrated items with fewer confirming checks and items recovering from earlier errors. Name-accuracy cases have a latest spelling slip, or repeated slips without three subsequent clean answers. Fragile cases have a recall gap among their last two forward appearances, even if the latest retry succeeded. These are transparent working rules, not a validated psychometric model. Earlier lesson and review evidence establishes the baseline; country-specific counts below cover cumulative rounds only.

Fewer checks means less confidence, not proof of less knowledge. A correct reverse answer does not establish forward retrieval. Multi-capital countries are grouped into one country row; counts span the tested roles. Spelling and actual forgetting should continue to receive different treatment. Recognition of a capital does not imply its disputed status is resolved.
