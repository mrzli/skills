# Writing tells

Patterns that make text read as generated. Adapted from `unslop` and `technical-writing` in [pstack](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/tree/main/pstack). Remove each one found, preserving meaning.

## Words

- Inflated vocabulary: crucial, pivotal, delve, enhance, foster, garner, intricate, showcase, testament, underscore, vibrant, leverage, utilize, facilitate, and abstract uses of landscape or tapestry. Use the plain word.
- Abstract metaphor nouns: substrate, surface (as in "API surface"), vector, wedge, nexus, paradigm, north star, flywheel, bedrock, and scaffolding or harness as metaphors. Name the concrete thing.
- "Serves as", "stands as", "boasts", or "features" where "is" or "has" fits.
- Adverbs propping up a weak verb, such as "significantly improves". Use a stronger verb or the measured number.
- Synonym cycling. Call each thing by one name throughout, and do not reword unchanged text between edits.

## Sentences

- Trailing -ing phrases that add vague significance: "…, ensuring reliability", "…, highlighting the need".
- Vague attributions: "experts believe", "it is widely considered". Name the source or cut the claim.
- Forced groups of three. Use the natural number of items.
- False ranges: "from X to Y" where X and Y are not on one scale.
- Colons used as mid-sentence connectors. Colons belong before a list or an example.
- Passive voice that hides who acts. Name the actor unless it is unknown or irrelevant.
- Pronouns with unclear referents. Each "it", "they", or "this" should point at one obvious noun. Repeat the noun when in doubt.
- Figurative phrasing and personified code, such as "the plan holds it" or "the value rides along". Say what happens literally.
- Over-compression: dropped articles, verbless fragments, arrows, and unexplained abbreviations. Write whole sentences.
- Sentences that could appear unchanged in another project's text. They say nothing specific, so cut or replace them with the concrete fact.

## Formatting and framing

- Bold label lines that restate the line, such as "**Performance:** Performance improved…". A bold lead-in followed by new detail is fine.
- Bold on ordinary terms, title-case headings, and decorative emojis.
- Framing labels: "the key insight", "at its core", "TL;DR", "the bottom line".
- Chatbot phrases and flattery: "Great question", "Certainly!", "I hope this helps", "Let me know if…".
- Generic closing statements that repeat or praise rather than inform.
