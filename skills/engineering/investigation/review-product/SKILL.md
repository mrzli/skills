name: review-product
description: Assess whether an existing product or feature helps its intended users achieve their goals. Use to evaluate usefulness, adoption, user friction, or product outcomes and recommend what to improve, investigate, or stop.
---

Evaluate product value against the intended user outcome. Keep this distinct from proving code correctness or assessing the development process.

## Establish the evaluation

Read the project brief, feature goals, prior evaluations, and available product evidence. Identify the audience, outcome, scope, and decision this review should inform. Reuse existing success criteria. If they are absent, propose observable criteria and label them as provisional.

Gather available evidence such as user feedback, support reports, usage data, task completion, and observed interaction with the product. Note the population, time period, collection method, and gaps that affect interpretation. For personal tools, the user's actual experience and representative tasks may be sufficient.

Use only available, authorized sources. A review request does not itself authorize contacting users, changing analytics, or exposing private user data. Summarize relevant patterns without retaining unnecessary personal information.

## Assess outcomes and friction

Trace important user journeys and compare intended outcomes with observed results. Separate reach or adoption problems from inability to complete a task, confusing behavior, and lack of value after completion. A requested feature is evidence of a need to understand, not automatic proof of the best solution.

Distinguish direct observations, user statements, aggregate measurements, and your own inferences. Treat a heuristic walkthrough as an assessment of likely usability issues, not evidence of real adoption or satisfaction. Avoid causal claims from correlations or conclusions unsupported by small or biased samples.

If outcome evidence is insufficient, explain what can be assessed and propose the smallest useful way to learn more. Do not manufacture metrics, user preferences, or validation to make the report appear complete.

## Recommend a decision

Identify the most important opportunities or reasons to retain the current product. For each recommendation, connect the evidence to a user problem, expected benefit, confidence, and cost or uncertainty. Consider simplifying or removing functionality as well as adding it.

Propose a concrete next step and how its effect would be evaluated. Distinguish a product decision from an experiment intended to inform one. Carry unresolved assumptions into any subsequent feature shaping or specification.

Return the findings and recommended decision, following the project's reporting convention. When a persistent report is useful or requested, save it to the established location or `.scratch/product-reviews/<descriptive-slug>.md`. Finish with evidence limitations and the next decision or learning step. Implementing product changes remains separate work.
